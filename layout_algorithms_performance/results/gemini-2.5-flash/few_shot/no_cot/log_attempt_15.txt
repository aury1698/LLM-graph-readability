To determine the minimum number of edges that must be removed to disconnect the two black nodes (M and B), we need to find the minimum edge cut between M and B. This is equivalent to finding the maximum number of edge-disjoint paths between M and B (by Menger's Theorem).

Let's list the direct neighbors of M and B:
- Neighbors of M: L, E, F, J
- Neighbors of B: E, G, H, C, N

We can identify several distinct "channels" or groups of paths from M to B:

**Channel 1: Paths through E**
- M - E - B
- M - L - E - B

**Channel 2: Paths through F**
- M - F - G - B
- M - F - K - G - B
- M - F - X - K - G - B
- M - F - G - H - B
- M - F - K - G - H - B
- M - F - X - K - G - H - B

**Channel 3: Paths through J**
- M - J - I - H - B
- M - J - I - C - B
- M - J - P - I - H - B
- M - J - P - I - C - B
- M - J - P - O - C - B
- M - J - P - O - N - B

Let's try to find a set of edges that, when removed, disconnects M from B.

**Consider a cut of 3 edges:**
1.  **Edges incident to M:** M has 4 incident edges: (M,L), (M,E), (M,F), (M,J). If we remove any 3 of these, one path from M to B will remain. For example, removing (M,L), (M,E), (M,F) leaves M connected via (M,J) to B (e.g., M-J-I-H-B). So, 3 edges incident to M are not enough.
2.  **Edges incident to B:** B has 5 incident edges: (B,E), (B,G), (B,H), (B,C), (B,N). If we remove any 3 of these, two paths from M to B will remain. For example, removing (B,E), (B,G), (B,H) leaves M connected via C and N (e.g., M-J-I-C-B and M-J-P-O-N-B). So, 3 edges incident to B are not enough.
3.  **Mixed edges:** Let's try to find a set of 3 "bottleneck" edges.
    *   If we remove (E,B), (G,B), (J,I):
        *   (E,B) cuts paths M-E-B and M-L-E-B.
        *   (G,B) cuts paths M-F-G-B, M-F-K-G-B, M-F-X-K-G-B.
        *   (J,I) cuts paths M-J-I-H-B and M-J-I-C-B.

        *   Also, paths like M-J-P-O-N-B (using (J,P), (P,O), (O,N), (N,B)) still exist.
        *   Therefore, this set of 3 edges is not enough.
