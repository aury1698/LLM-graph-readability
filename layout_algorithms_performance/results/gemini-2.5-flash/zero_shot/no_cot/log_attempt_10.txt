    *   Remaining neighbors of H to explore: None (G paths use F, I paths are already used or will use nodes from Path 2).
    *   Remaining neighbors of E to explore: B.
    *   Let's try to find a path from H to E that uses B, and is disjoint from F, I, J, M, L.
    *   Consider the path: **H - I - C - B - E** (Nodes involved: I, C, B). This path uses I, which is in Path 2. So, this path is not node-disjoint from Path 2.

This indicates that finding 3 node-disjoint paths is difficult, and perhaps not possible. Let's try to find a cut set directly.

Consider the direct neighbors of E: {B, L, F}. If we remove these three nodes, E will be completely isolated from the rest of the graph, and thus from H.

Let's verify if removing {B, L, F} disconnects H from E:
1.  **H - F - E**: Cut by removing F.
2.  **Paths starting with H - G**: All paths from G to E must pass through F. Since F is removed, all H-G paths are cut.
3.  **Paths starting with H - I**:
    *   H - I - J - M - L - E: Cut by removing L.
    *   H - I - J - P - O - N - B - E: Cut by removing B.
    *   H - I - J - P - O - C - B - E: Cut by removing B.
    *   H - I - O - N - B - E: Cut by removing B.
    *   H - I - O - C - B - E: Cut by removing B.
    *   H - I - C - N - B - E: Cut by removing B.
    *   H - I - C - O - N - B - E: Cut by removing B.
    *   H - I - C - O - P - J - M - L - E: Cut by removing L.

All paths from H to E are indeed cut by removing the set {B, L, F}. This is a cut set of 3 nodes.

To confirm this is the *minimum* number, we need to show that 2 nodes are not sufficient.
If 2 nodes were sufficient, it would mean there are at most 2 node-disjoint paths.
Let's re-examine the paths for node-disjointness.
We established that all paths from H-G to E must use F.
So, if we remove F, all H-G paths are cut.
This means that if we remove F, we only need to disconnect H-I from E.

Let's consider the subgraph where F is removed. We need to disconnect I from E.
Neighbors of I: {J, O, C}
Neighbors of E: {B, L} (F is removed)

Paths from I to E (without F):
*   I - J - M - L - E (Nodes: J, M, L)
*   I - O - N - B - E (Nodes: O, N, B)
These two paths (I-J-M-L-E and I-O-N-B-E) are node-disjoint (J, M, L are distinct from O, N, B).
Since there are at least two node-disjoint paths from I to E (in the subgraph without F), by Menger's theorem, we need to remove at least 2 nodes to disconnect I from E.
For example, removing {B, L} would disconnect I from E.

Therefore, the total minimum number of nodes to remove is 1 (for F) + 2 (for I to E) = 3 nodes.
The cut set {F, B, L} is indeed a minimum cut set.

Nodes to remove: 3, Cut Set: [B, L, F]
